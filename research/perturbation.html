Sample size for single-cell perturbation screens

Closed-form formulas for how many cells are needed to estimate a drug's perturbation direction to a given precision.

Status
Under review, Biostatistics
Period
Feb – Oct 2026
Methods
Delta method, asymptotic variance, covariance propagation
  1. The problem
  2. Approach
  3. Manuscript

The problem

Single-cell perturbation screens measure how a drug or genetic perturbation shifts a cell's expression profile. Often the quantity of interest is the direction of that shift in a low-dimensional space, not its size. Biological and technical noise make that direction uncertain, and experimenters need to know how many cells to sequence before running the screen.

Approach

The direction is a smooth function of the mean shift, so its uncertainty can be approximated with the multivariate delta method:

Var(θ̂) ≈ ∇g(μ)⊤ Σ ∇g(μ) / n
  • A first-order Taylor expansion links the covariance of the mean shift to the variance of the estimated direction.
  • Inverting that relation gives a closed-form sample size for a target precision, driven by the signal-to-noise ratio of the perturbation.
  • Simulations check how biological and technical noise affect directional precision and drug-response signals.

Manuscript

“Closed-form sample size calculation for estimating perturbation directions in single-cell screens.” Under review at Biostatistics. A link will be added when it is published.
